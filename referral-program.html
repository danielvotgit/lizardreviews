Sooner or later, someone you work with says the sentence: "We got a fake review and Google will not remove it." A web designer hears it from a client. An accountant hears it over a quarterly review. A business owner hears it from a friend who runs the shop next door. Right now that sentence earns you nothing. With this program it earns 10%.

We run a pure-play Google review removal service. We accept or decline a case within 24 hours, removal usually lands within 24 to 48 hours, and the client pays only after Google confirms the review is gone. Because the client carries no upfront risk, it is an easy introduction to make, and you stay out of the delivery entirely.

How does the referral program work?

  1. Register the introduction. Fill in the form below with the business name and a contact email. If you have the link to the review, add it. It takes about a minute.
  2. We confirm within 24 hours. You get an email saying whether the referral counts. It is turned down only if the business is already our client or already talking to us.
  3. We take it from there. We contact the business, tell them you made the introduction, and review the case. We accept or decline within 24 hours. You do not pitch, price or support anything.
  4. They pay, you earn. The business pays after Google confirms removal. Your 10% is paid once that money has reached us.

Who should refer?

The program suits anyone who is regularly close to a business owner with a reputation problem:

  • Web designers, SEO and marketing freelancers whose clients have a Google Business Profile.
  • Accountants, bookkeepers and business consultants who hear about the problem first.
  • Business owners and managers with friends and peers in local trades and services.
  • Agencies that would rather introduce than resell. If you want to own the client and set your own price, the partner arrangements are the better fit, and those are priced on a call.

Run a large network, an association or a high-volume agency? Ask us about a custom arrangement instead of the standard 10%.

What makes a good referral?

A good referral is a business with a live Google review it believes breaks Google's content policies: a fake, a competitor attack, an ex-employee's revenge, a review from someone who was never a customer, a coordinated wave, or a review attached to an extortion demand. The review needs text, because we cannot remove rating-only reviews (a star with no words).

One honest limit. We do not remove truthful, unfavorable reviews, and we decline cases that do not look like policy violations. A decline is free for the client and pays no commission, so send us the borderline ones anyway and let us make the call.

What are the rules, in plain English?

  • You earn commission only on cases we accept and the client pays for.
  • Referring your own business or your own staff does not count.
  • Tell the business owner you are introducing them to us before you register them.
  • If you promote us publicly, say clearly that you may earn a commission.
  • Do not promise outcomes we have not promised, and do not claim any inside connection at Google.
  • Do not run paid ads on our brand name.
  • Attorneys and other regulated professionals must check that they may accept a referral fee at all.
