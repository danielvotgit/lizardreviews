Most owners handle a bad review the same way. They reread it three times, flag it, maybe write a reply, and then leave it and hope it sinks out of sight. It does not. It sits on your profile, and almost everyone who compares local businesses reads reviews: BrightLocal's 2025 Local Consumer Review Survey found that only 4% of consumers say they never read online business reviews.

The longer you wait, the more it costs. That holds for the sales you lose, for the number of reviews you end up clearing, and for how much of the original story you can still prove.

Do Google reviews expire?

No. Google has no age limit on reviews. A review stays on your profile until the reviewer deletes it or Google takes it down for breaking policy. Waiting a year for it to sort itself out changes nothing about the review. It only adds a year to the time it has been working against you.

Is it cheaper to act early?

Yes, in total. Our direct price is $450 per review, billed only after Google confirms removal. Acting early shrinks what surrounds that fee, in three ways.

  1. Fewer reviews to remove. We bill $450 per review. Bad reviews often arrive in groups, for example after a billing dispute, as in this urgent care case. Each review is billed on its own, so a pile that built up while you waited costs more to clear than the first review would have.
  2. Fresher records. A case rests on what you can show: the invoice, the booking, the order, the email thread. Right after a review lands, all of it is a search away. A year later the files are archived, staff have moved on, and nobody remembers the customer.
  3. Less time on the free route. Flagging costs nothing, so most owners start there. As our turnaround data shows, DIY flagging takes anywhere from 2 days to 6 weeks and support escalation 2 to 8 weeks, against 24 to 48 hours at Lizard Reviews and usually 4 to 30 days with other removal services. Flag once if you like. If it comes back denied, send us the link that day instead of appealing for another month.

What should you do the day a bad review lands?

  1. Copy the link and take a screenshot. Get the link to the review from Google Maps. If the reviewer edits or deletes the text later, you still have the original.
  2. Pull the records. Find the invoice, booking, order or email thread that shows who this person was, or that no such customer exists in your files.
  3. Hold the public reply. A reply written on the day you are angry rarely helps. You can answer once the removal request is in.
  4. Send it to us. Fill in the form on the homepage with your name, email, the review link and a sentence about the business. We tell you within 24 hours whether we take the case, and you are billed only after Google confirms removal.

What if new bad reviews keep coming?

Some businesses see bad reviews every month: restaurants, clinics, home services, and the agencies that manage them. Do not save them up for a quarterly cleanup. Send each one on the day it appears, using the same form.
